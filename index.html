How the intelligence builds

The system keeps direct evidence, calculated exposure and transmission mechanisms separate so a user can see exactly where each conclusion comes from.
01 · DIRECT EVIDENCE

What is measured or modelled?

  • Outdoor heat conditions and warm nights
  • River-flood depth by return period
  • Rainfall extremes and wet/dry spells
  • DSM elevation and local surface context
02 · SECOND ORDER

What is exposed?

  • Workers × heat days
  • Workers, machinery or capacity at inundated sites
  • Locally low surface position within a flood-exposed area
  • Asset concentration in the same hazard footprint
03 · THIRD ORDER

How can disruption propagate?

  • Shared road and port bottlenecks
  • Common borrower/collateral exposure
  • Correlated insurer accumulation
  • Supply-chain and infrastructure dependence

Worker heat exposure

1,500 workers × 24 illustrative extreme-heat days = 36,000 worker-heat exposure days.

Exposure arithmetic only. It is not an estimate of lost workdays, productivity loss or health outcomes.

Flood + local surface position

The synthetic site is inside the illustrative RP100 flood footprint and sits 1.8 m below its surrounding 500 m DSM median.

This combination adds context; it does not by itself establish drainage failure, floor inundation, damage or expected loss.

Evidence and provenance

Every production result is designed to retain source type, vintage, method version, quality state and processing lineage.

What this prototype establishes

Physical hazard evidence can be attached to defensibly geocoded assets and then connected to operational scale, route dependence and financial exposure without hiding the chain of reasoning.

What it does not establish

Heat, flood, rainfall or DSM evidence alone does not determine downtime, damage, default probability, collateral haircut, insurance loss or investment suitability.
